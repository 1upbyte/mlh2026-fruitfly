Why it matters

A whole brain you can read, synapse by synapse

139,255Neurons in the mapped fly brain
54.5MSynapses between them

The adult fruit fly brain was mapped in full in 2024, and the wiring is free to download. Smellcheck runs the 9,039 neurons that smell and learn.

Source: FlyWire consortium, Nature, October 2024.

Chat moderation is word lists or black boxes.

A word list misses anything it has not seen. A large language model catches more, but nobody can say why it banned you.

A fly learns which smells to avoid from reward and punishment, in a circuit small enough to inspect. We asked whether that circuit can learn a job it never evolved for.

How it works

How a word becomes a smell

→ →

A pretrained text model embeds each word. PCA reduces the embedding to 53 features, which set the firing rates of the simulated fly's smell receptors.

We lowercase chat, shorten repeated letters, and score each word separately. The 53 rates drive 2,279 receptor neurons, and about 8% of the 5,177 Kenyon cells respond to any one word.

How it works

Training the smell circuit

9,039Simulated neurons
200 msBrain time per word

The circuit uses wiring adapted from the fly connectome.

We compare activity feeding the approach and avoidance outputs to calculate a smell score from 1 to 10.

Reward and punishment change the response.

During training, dopamine signals adjust the active connections between Kenyon cells and output neurons.

Only those 62,261 synapses learn. The rest of the adapted circuit stays fixed.

Training run: 1,842 of 3,343 labeled words, 12 epochs, about 12 minutes on one GPU. The other 1,501 words are held out for testing.

How it works

The worst word decides

If a word scores 4.5 or above, use the highest score.

Otherwise, a word at 1.5 or below earns the message a Mod Award. Failing both, use the average word score.

1 means pleasant. 10 means foul. We check the action thresholds from highest to lowest.

Unavailable word scores default to 3. The model does not read sentence context.

How it works

Applying the verdict to chat

  1. POST /api/fly/smellThe server returns cached word scores or asks the trained Python model.
  2. The browser calculates the message score and sends its action to the moderation API when the message appears.
  3. The server pushes state updates to open tabs. Ban, mute, and timeout hide the user's messages in chat and on fly monitors. Mod Awards pause playback.

The flies divide the chat.

Each displayed fly gets every Nth message. With three flies:

All displayed flies share one trained model. The video clock controls when chat messages appear.

Results

It learned good from bad, on words it never smelled

71%Good / neutral / bad accuracy on 354 held-out words
33%Same test, answering "neutral" every time
48 / 50Of its 50 best-rated words are labeled kind
7 / 10Of its 10 worst-rated words were never taught

We had the fly rate all 3,343 labeled words. Its favorites include excellent, grateful and lovely. Encouraging, peace and happy rank near the top too, and it was never taught those. Seven of its ten worst are slurs or hate terms.

Training: the smell error on the dictionary fell from 2.00 to 1.35 over 12 epochs.

Results

It cannot tell bad from worse

354 held-out words, scale 1 to 10. Every bad word lands between 5.6 and 6.9.

On its own the fly almost never bans: it catches 11% of ban-worthy words. A plain linear model on the same 53 inputs scores 83% where the fly scores 71%.

So the fly wears a harness.

It stretches the fly's scale, keeps known slurs at Mute or worse, and caps words nobody has labeled at a Warn.

Left alone on 140 xQc chat messages, the fly banned "LOOOOOLLL" and gave 12 timeouts. With the harness: no bans, 3 timeouts, all for real swearing, and 4 Mod Awards. It still warns 53 times.

What it means

A mapped brain can learn a job it never evolved for

Only the fly's own learning rule was used.

Dopamine changed 62,261 synapses. Everything else is the wiring as mapped, apart from three corrections that stop the circuit saturating.

It learned what this circuit is for.

Approach or avoid is what a fly's smell circuit computes, and it got that right 71% of the time on unseen words. A five-step punishment ladder is not, which is why the harness exists.

Biology did not beat arithmetic.

A linear model does better. The value is that a real connectome did it at all, at 0.16 seconds a word on a laptop, and that every verdict traces back to named neurons.

Next: sentence context instead of single words, and a severity signal the fly can learn.
